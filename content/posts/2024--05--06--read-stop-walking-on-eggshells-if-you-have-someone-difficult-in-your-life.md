As a Licensed Professional Counselor, I highly recommend reading [Stop Walking on Eggshells](<https://www.amazon.com/s?k=the+stop+walking+on+eggshells&i=stripbooks&hvadid=633846247908&hvdev=c&hvlocphy=9028233&hvnetw=g&hvqmt=e&hvrand=15301725491421708553&hvtargid=kwd-314460752307&hydadcr=15529_13558536&tag=googhydr-20&ref=pd_sl_zrtbhajl5_e>) by Paul T. T. Mason MS and Randi Kreger if you have anyone difficult in your life.  This book is so influential, it's listed as my #1 best resource.

Frequently I think to myself, "I should buy this book by the caseload and just give it as a gift to my clients."  This insightful read is really *that good!*  And, the improvement seen in the relationships of my clients as a result of understanding the context of Stop Walking on Eggshells has been very impressive.

Stop Walking on Eggshells is for anyone who has a difficult person in their life who keeps doing damage in interpersonal relationships, then refuses to take accountability for their actions.  In clear language, the authors help to demystify why a person acts in such a way, and most importantly, gives tips and tools on how to set boundaries with your difficult person and bring sanity back into your life.

You, or your loved one, does not need to have a Borderline Personality Disorder diagnosis to find this book helpful.  Actionable steps, detailed explanations about brain processing, and behavior management integrate throughout to help you be more fulfilled in your relationship.

Consistently I receive feedback from clients about how they see themselves or a loved one in the patient stories shared throughout Stop Walking on Eggshells.  The testimonials are relatable and provide examples which I'm sure will make you feel less alone.  Whether facing someone with Borderline Personality Disorder or not, you are absolutely not alone in your journey.

If you need to go the next step and speak with a professional, seek out a referral, or if you're in Texas, I'd be happy to be of assistance.  You deserve positive interpersonal relationships, and through therapy and resources such as Stop Walking on Eggshells, may help you realize that.
